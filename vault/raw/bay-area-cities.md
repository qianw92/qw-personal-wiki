# Major Bay Area Cities

Reference notes on the San Francisco Bay Area's major cities: population, location relative to San Francisco and Berkeley, and one key highlight per city. Population figures are from the **2020 U.S. Census** (see Sources below) and were cross-checked against the "List of cities and towns in the San Francisco Bay Area" Wikipedia page. Grouped by sub-region for orientation.

## San Francisco Peninsula & City

### San Francisco
- Population: 873,965 (2020 Census).
- Location: the Bay Area's anchor city, at the northern tip of the San Francisco Peninsula, on a peninsula bordered by the Pacific Ocean to the west and San Francisco Bay to the east. All "north/south/east/west of SF" references in this note set are measured from here.
- Highlight: dense, hilly, and historically the region's financial, cultural, and tourism center, home to the Golden Gate Bridge and a compact 7x7-mile street grid.

### Daly City
- Population: 104,901 (2020 Census).
- Location: immediately south of San Francisco, the first city encountered driving south along the Peninsula on Highway 1 or I-280.
- Highlight: known for its foggy hillside neighborhoods and as a major gateway suburb connecting SF to the rest of the Peninsula.

### South San Francisco
- Population: 66,105 (2020 Census).
- Location: south of San Francisco and just south of Daly City, at the northern end of the Peninsula's biotech corridor.
- Highlight: nicknamed "The Industrial City," it is now a hub for biotechnology companies, with a giant hillside sign reading "South San Francisco The Industrial City."

### San Mateo
- Population: 105,661 (2020 Census).
- Location: on the Peninsula, south of San Francisco and south of South San Francisco, roughly halfway between SF and Palo Alto.
- Highlight: a central Peninsula suburb with a walkable downtown and the San Mateo Bridge connecting east to the East Bay.

### Redwood City
- Population: 84,292 (2020 Census).
- Location: on the Peninsula, south of San Mateo and north of Palo Alto.
- Highlight: home to a major Oracle campus (Oracle's corporate headquarters relocated to Austin, Texas in December 2020).

### Menlo Park
- Population: 33,780 (2020 Census).
- Location: on the Peninsula, south of Redwood City and just north of Palo Alto, bordering East Palo Alto.
- Highlight: headquarters of Meta (Facebook) and adjacent to Stanford University's land holdings.

### Palo Alto
- Population: 68,572 (2020 Census).
- Location: on the Peninsula, south of Menlo Park, directly across San Francisco Bay from the East Bay city of Fremont.
- Highlight: home to Stanford University and considered a birthplace of Silicon Valley's startup culture.

## South Bay / Silicon Valley

### Mountain View
- Population: 82,376 (2020 Census).
- Location: south of Palo Alto, on the southwest edge of the Bay, between the Peninsula and the South Bay.
- Highlight: headquarters of Google (Alphabet), with a downtown Castro Street known for tech-worker foot traffic.

### Sunnyvale
- Population: 155,805 (2020 Census).
- Location: south of Mountain View, between Mountain View and Santa Clara.
- Highlight: a major Silicon Valley tech employment center, historically home to defense and semiconductor firms before today's software companies.

### Cupertino
- Population: 60,381 (2020 Census).
- Location: southwest of Sunnyvale, in the western South Bay near the base of the Santa Cruz Mountains.
- Highlight: headquarters of Apple, including the ring-shaped Apple Park campus.

### Santa Clara
- Population: 127,647 (2020 Census).
- Location: directly north of San Jose, between Sunnyvale and San Jose.
- Highlight: home to Levi's Stadium (the San Francisco 49ers' home field) and Santa Clara University.

### San Jose
- Population: 1,013,240 (2020 Census).
- Location: at the southern end of San Francisco Bay, well south of SF and generally considered the South Bay's largest city and the unofficial "capital" of Silicon Valley.
- Highlight: the most populous city in the Bay Area and the third-most populous in California, though historically overshadowed in name recognition by San Francisco.

### Milpitas
- Population: 80,273 (2020 Census).
- Location: northeast of San Jose, at the northern tip of the South Bay along the eastern shore.
- Highlight: a smaller tech-manufacturing hub with one of the Bay Area's largest shopping malls, the Great Mall.

## East Bay

### Oakland
- Population: 440,646 (2020 Census).
- Location: directly across San Francisco Bay from SF (east of SF), reachable via the Bay Bridge; immediately south of Berkeley.
- Highlight: the East Bay's largest city, with a working port, a diverse population, and its own distinct downtown and arts scene separate from SF.

### Berkeley
- Population: 124,321 (2020 Census).
- Location: on the east shore of the Bay, north of Oakland and directly across the Bay from SF and the Golden Gate.
- Highlight: home to the University of California, Berkeley, and a long history as a center of activism and academic research.

### Alameda
- Population: 78,280 (2020 Census).
- Location: an island city just south of Oakland, connected by bridges and a tunnel, across the estuary from Oakland's downtown.
- Highlight: a former naval air station site, now known for its preserved Victorian-era housing stock and a small-town feel close to Oakland.

### Richmond
- Population: 116,448 (2020 Census).
- Location: on the east shore of the Bay, north of Berkeley, connected to Marin County by the Richmond-San Rafael Bridge.
- Highlight: an industrial port city with a major oil refinery and growing waterfront redevelopment.

### Fremont
- Population: 230,504 (2020 Census).
- Location: at the southeastern edge of the Bay, south of Oakland/Hayward, directly across the Bay from Palo Alto and Menlo Park.
- Highlight: home to Tesla's primary vehicle assembly plant and a large, diverse South Asian immigrant community.

### Hayward
- Population: 162,954 (2020 Census).
- Location: on the east shore of the Bay, between Oakland/San Leandro to the north and Fremont to the south, connected to the Peninsula by the San Mateo Bridge.
- Highlight: a historically industrial and agricultural city, now a mid-Bay residential hub with California State University, East Bay.

### Walnut Creek
- Population: 70,127 (2020 Census).
- Location: inland East Bay, northeast of Oakland and Berkeley, over the hills from the Bay shoreline.
- Highlight: a suburban retail and dining hub for the inland East Bay ("Contra Costa County"), less foggy than the Bay-facing cities.

### Concord
- Population: 125,410 (2020 Census).
- Location: inland East Bay, north of Walnut Creek, further from SF than most Bay-shore cities.
- Highlight: the largest city in Contra Costa County, a commuter suburb with a large central park and amphitheater.

### Pleasanton
- Population: 79,871 (2020 Census).
- Location: inland East Bay, southeast of Oakland and Hayward, near the junction of I-580 and I-680.
- Highlight: a corporate office hub (e.g., Workday) with a preserved historic downtown Main Street.

### Livermore
- Population: 87,955 (2020 Census).
- Location: the easternmost major Bay Area city, east of Pleasanton, at the edge of the Bay Area before the Central Valley.
- Highlight: home to Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory and a growing wine-country region (the Livermore Valley).

## North Bay

### San Rafael
- Population: 61,271 (2020 Census).
- Location: in Marin County, north of San Francisco across the Golden Gate Bridge, the largest city in Marin.
- Highlight: Marin County's government and commercial center, at the base of hills leading to Mount Tamalpais.

### Vallejo
- Population: 126,090 (2020 Census).
- Location: at the northern tip of San Francisco Bay, where it meets the Carquinez Strait, northeast of Richmond across the water.
- Highlight: a historic naval shipyard town (Mare Island Naval Shipyard, decommissioned 1996), now a more affordable North Bay commuter city.

### Napa
- Population: 79,246 (2020 Census).
- Location: north of Vallejo, at the southern end of the Napa Valley wine region.
- Highlight: the commercial hub of Napa Valley, world-famous for wine tourism.

### Santa Rosa
- Population: 178,127 (2020 Census).
- Location: in Sonoma County, northwest of Napa and the largest city in the North Bay.
- Highlight: the North Bay's commercial and government center, at the heart of Sonoma County's wine and agricultural region.
