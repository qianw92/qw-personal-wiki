# San Francisco Neighborhoods

Reference notes on San Francisco's major neighborhoods, focused on where each one sits relative to the city as a whole. San Francisco is a roughly 7x7-mile square peninsula tip; directions below are relative to downtown/Market Street unless noted, with the Pacific Ocean to the west, San Francisco Bay to the north and east, and the rest of the Peninsula to the south.

## Northeast SF (waterfront and historic core)

### Financial District
- Location: northeast SF, the compact high-rise core just south of the Embarcadero waterfront.
- Highlight: the region's traditional banking and corporate headquarters district, and the densest cluster of skyscrapers in the city.

### North Beach
- Location: north-central SF, just north of the Financial District and east of Russian Hill.
- Highlight: the city's historic Italian-American neighborhood, known for cafes, and adjacent to Chinatown.

### Chinatown
- Location: north-central SF, just north of Union Square and west of the Financial District.
- Highlight: one of the oldest and most established Chinatowns in North America, dense with shops and residences.

### Nob Hill
- Location: north-central SF, on a hilltop just west of the Financial District and northeast of Union Square.
- Highlight: historically home to the city's wealthiest 19th-century residents, known for grand hotels and cable car lines.

### Russian Hill
- Location: north SF, west of North Beach and north of Nob Hill, close to the northern waterfront.
- Highlight: known for its steep, winding streets, including the famous switchback block of Lombard Street.

### Telegraph Hill
- Location: northeast SF, east of North Beach, overlooking the northern waterfront and Bay.
- Highlight: topped by Coit Tower, with views over the Financial District and the Bay Bridge.

## Central SF

### Union Square
- Location: central-north SF, just south of the Financial District's western edge.
- Highlight: the city's main shopping and theater district, centered on a public plaza.

### Tenderloin
- Location: central SF, just west of Union Square and southeast of Nob Hill.
- Highlight: a dense, historically low-income neighborhood in the geographic middle of downtown, with significant social services.

### Hayes Valley
- Location: central SF, west of Civic Center and north of the Mission District.
- Highlight: a boutique shopping and dining strip that emerged after a freeway removal opened up the area in the 1990s.

### Civic Center
- Location: central SF, south of the Tenderloin, around SF's City Hall.
- Highlight: the city and county government center, including City Hall, main library, and performing arts venues.

## South of Market and Eastern SF

### SoMa (South of Market)
- Location: central-east SF, immediately south of Market Street and the Financial District.
- Highlight: a former industrial area now home to tech company offices, convention space (Moscone Center), and museums.

### Mission Bay
- Location: southeast SF, south of SoMa along the eastern waterfront.
- Highlight: a newer, purpose-built district anchored by UCSF's biomedical campus and the Chase Center arena.

### Potrero Hill
- Location: southeast SF, south of Mission Bay, on a hill east of the Mission District.
- Highlight: a sunnier hillside neighborhood with sweeping Bay views, historically industrial at its base.

### Dogpatch
- Location: southeast SF, along the waterfront south of Mission Bay and east of Potrero Hill.
- Highlight: a former shipbuilding and industrial area now known for converted warehouse spaces and galleries.

## Central-South SF

### The Mission (Mission District)
- Location: south-central SF, south of the Tenderloin/Hayes Valley area and east of the Castro.
- Highlight: a historically Latino neighborhood known for murals, taquerias, and one of the city's sunniest microclimates.

### The Castro
- Location: south-central SF, west of the Mission District and south of Hayes Valley/Duboce Triangle.
- Highlight: internationally known as the center of San Francisco's LGBTQ+ community and history.

### Noe Valley
- Location: south-central SF, directly south of the Castro.
- Highlight: a quieter, family-oriented neighborhood with a village-like main shopping street (24th Street).

### Twin Peaks
- Location: geographic center of SF, west of Noe Valley and the Castro.
- Highlight: two hilltop summits offering the most panoramic views of the entire city and Bay.

### Excelsior
- Location: south-central SF, south of the Mission District, bordered by John McLaren Park to the east.
- Highlight: a diverse, largely residential working-class neighborhood in the city's southern flatlands, centered along Mission Street.

## Western SF

### Pacific Heights
- Location: north SF, west of Russian Hill and north of Japantown, on a ridge overlooking the Bay.
- Highlight: one of the city's wealthiest neighborhoods, known for grand mansions and Bay views.

### The Marina
- Location: north SF, along the northern waterfront, west of Russian Hill and the Presidio's eastern edge.
- Highlight: a flat, waterfront neighborhood with views of the Golden Gate Bridge, popular for jogging and yacht harbors.

### The Presidio
- Location: northwest corner of SF, at the foot of the Golden Gate Bridge.
- Highlight: a former U.S. Army post, now a national park with forests, trails, and coastal bluffs.

### Richmond District (Inner & Outer)
- Location: northwest SF, north of Golden Gate Park, stretching from the park to the Pacific Ocean.
- Highlight: a foggy, residential district with a large Asian-American population and Clement Street's shopping corridor.

### Sunset District (Inner & Outer)
- Location: southwest SF, south of Golden Gate Park, stretching from the park to the Pacific Ocean.
- Highlight: the city's largest residential district, known for foggy weather, rows of similar houses, and Ocean Beach.

### Golden Gate Park
- Location: west-central SF, spanning the width of the city between the Richmond and Sunset districts.
- Highlight: a large landscaped park (bigger than New York's Central Park) with museums, gardens, and open lawns, forming the boundary between the northwest and southwest neighborhoods.
