# Bay Area Sub-Regions Overview

A short reference note defining the Bay Area's main sub-regions and how cities are typically grouped into them. Useful for orienting any city or neighborhood relative to the region as a whole.

## The five main sub-regions

- **San Francisco / The Peninsula**: San Francisco sits at the northern tip of a peninsula bounded by the Pacific Ocean (west) and San Francisco Bay (east). "The Peninsula" usually refers to the string of cities south of SF along that strip, from Daly City down through Palo Alto, before reaching the South Bay.
- **South Bay / Silicon Valley**: the cities ringing the southern end of San Francisco Bay, roughly from Mountain View and Sunnyvale through San Jose. This is the Bay Area's largest sub-region by population and the historic center of the tech industry.
- **East Bay**: the cities on the eastern shore of the Bay, reached from SF via the Bay Bridge. Divided informally into the Bay-facing cities (Oakland, Berkeley, Alameda, Richmond) and the inland Contra Costa County cities further east (Walnut Creek, Concord, Pleasanton, Livermore).
- **North Bay**: Marin, Sonoma, and Napa Counties, north of San Francisco across the Golden Gate Bridge or north of Richmond across the Richmond-San Rafael Bridge. Includes San Rafael, Novato, Vallejo, Napa, Santa Rosa, and Petaluma.
- **Peninsula-to-South Bay corridor**: sometimes Redwood City, Menlo Park, Palo Alto, Mountain View, and Sunnyvale are grouped together as the corridor connecting the Peninsula to Silicon Valley proper, since the Bay narrows and the two areas blend together there.

## Orientation reference points

- Directions "north/south of San Francisco" in this wiki are measured along the Peninsula and North Bay axis, with San Francisco itself as zero point.
- Directions "north/south of Berkeley" refer to the East Bay's Bay-facing shoreline, where Richmond sits north of Berkeley and Oakland sits south of Berkeley.
- The Bay is crossed by several bridges: the Golden Gate Bridge (SF to Marin/North Bay), the Bay Bridge (SF to Oakland/East Bay), the San Mateo Bridge (Peninsula to Hayward/East Bay), the Dumbarton Bridge (Peninsula to Fremont/East Bay), and the Richmond-San Rafael Bridge (East Bay to Marin/North Bay).
- San Francisco Bay narrows to nothing at its southern end near San Jose, which is why the South Bay cities (San Jose, Santa Clara, Fremont) are considered to "close the loop" between the Peninsula and the East Bay.
